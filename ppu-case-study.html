The brief

OLX's package model was costing the business its best sellers

High-volume sellers on OLX — car dealers, parts traders, seasonal listers — were churning at an accelerating rate. The cause: a billing model that forced them to predict monthly ad volume upfront, then punished them for being wrong. Simultaneously, users migrating from OLX's vertical platforms (Otomoto in PL, Standvirtual in PT, Autovit in RO) expected pay-per-use billing as standard. Forcing them into packages risked losing $889k/month in migration revenue at the exact moment of transition.

Assumption mapping

Riskiest assumptions surfaced before fieldwork

The full product brief was fed to Claude to extract implicit assumptions and rank by failure risk — turning brief review into a structured pre-research checklist rather than a passive read.

Claude analysis Risk ranking
Self-imposed posting cap
Sellers stop posting when packages run out because spending more feels like a loss — which means PPU must make unlimited posting feel safe, not just fair. Backed by −24.4% listings data.
Vertical user muscle memory
Migrating sellers expect PPU as standard. Any OLX-specific UX friction risks $889k/month in the critical migration window. Familiarity is a feature — over-engineering is a risk.
Discount confusion destroys trust
All 10 research participants noticed discount offers but could not explain what activated them or what period they covered. Discount mechanics excluded from MVP scope as a result.
② Define Converge — frame the right problem

From 62 interviews to a problem statement in one afternoon

NotebookLM outputs were fed into Claude for insight structuring. 80+ raw research notes were clustered into themes, upgraded into design insights using a structured format, and ranked by business severity. HMW questions were generated, categorised, and stress-tested before any solution work began.

Problem statement

High-volume postpaid sellers need a monetisation model that accommodates unpredictable inventory volumes without financial waste or operational friction — because the current package model forces them to predict and commit to the unpredictable.

How Might We questions

5 HMWs drove the entire Develop phase

Generated from insights, grouped by theme, and stress-tested by asking Claude to argue against each one before adopting them.

HMW let sellers post freely without committing to volume upfront?
HMW make bracket pricing transparent before the first ad is posted?
HMW make sellers want to post more to unlock cheaper rates?
AI process note

Two-tool research stack: NotebookLM → Claude

NotebookLM handled source-grounded qualitative analysis — staying tethered to actual transcripts, surfacing quotes, preventing confabulation. Claude handled the forward-looking design work — structuring insights, generating HMWs, drafting problem statements. The manual handoff between tools was intentional: that moment of copying findings is where editorial judgment enters the process.

③ Develop Diverge — explore, prototype, stress-test

A 4-hour engineering ideation session that produced 7 working concepts

Rather than a traditional design sprint, the Develop phase used a cross-functional Lovable workshop — designers and engineers ideating together through AI-generated prototypes in real time. 7 distinct UX directions were built, presented, and dot-voted in one session. Claude Design was used for higher-fidelity mockup iterations after the workshop.

7 concepts built and voted on in a single session

Pricing page, posting flow, usage dashboard, and single-improvement concepts were prototyped live. Each team member built independently then presented. Dot voting surfaced the interactive calculator and bracket-progress dashboard as the strongest directions.

Prototyping · Claude Design + Claude Artifacts

Working pricing calculator built in hours

A functional React PPU calculator — with bracket breakdown, tier selection, live cost comparison, and logarithmic volume scale up to 100k ads — was built as a Claude Artifact and used directly in user testing sessions as an interactive stimulus.

Claude Design Claude Artifacts React prototype
Concept selected

Postpaid PPU with bracket pricing + benefit tiers

Sellers choose Basic, Premium, or Mega tier (listing quality). Post unlimited ads. Billed at month-end on incremental brackets. No expiry. No upfront commitment.

Key UX decision

Progress-to-next-bracket as a motivational mechanic

Usage dashboard shows how many more ads until the next cheaper rate unlocks — turning billing transparency into a posting incentive. Highest dot-voted concept in the workshop.

Stress-tested by Claude

Auto-extend disclosure surfaced as a legal risk

Claude flagged that auto-extended ads under variable pricing require explicit labelling under B2B regulations. Design constraint added to Phase 5 scope before it could become a launch blocker.

④ Deliver Converge — validate and hand off

Validated across 3 markets, with AI-assisted test synthesis

Usability sessions were run across PL, PT, and RO with the working calculator as the primary test stimulus. Observer notes were synthesised in Claude after each round — pattern detection across sessions that previously took a day of affinity mapping was reduced to under an hour.

Key finding from testing

Cost anxiety blocks free posting without a spend cap

7 of 9 test sessions surfaced anxiety about open-ended billing. An optional monthly spend cap — not in the original brief — was added as a feature. This single finding from test synthesis directly changed the product spec.

Old process vs AI-accelerated process

The same diamond — fundamentally different speed and depth

The double diamond didn't change. The four phases, the diverge-converge rhythm, the designer's judgment — all stayed the same. What changed was how much ground could be covered inside each phase, and how much time was freed up for the thinking that only a human can do.

Honest reflection

What AI changed — and what it didn't

The most important design decisions in this project were not made by AI. What changed was the speed and coverage of everything surrounding those decisions.

Where AI had real leverage

Synthesised 62 interviews into ranked design insights in under an hour — what previously took a full-day affinity workshop
Generated 3× more concept directions in the ideation phase, making the narrowing decision feel informed rather than arbitrary
Built a working interactive prototype (pricing calculator) in hours, not a developer ticket weeks away
Surfaced the auto-extend legal risk and the cost anxiety finding before they became blockers
Encoded the entire methodology as a reusable Project — consistent outputs, zero re-explaining, shareable with team

What I owned as the designer

Deciding which insights were actually load-bearing and which were noise
The problem statement — Claude generated 5 drafts, I chose the one I'd stand behind
Stakeholder alignment across product, finance, legal, and commercial teams
The judgment to add the spend cap when 7/9 sessions flagged cost anxiety — AI detected the pattern, I decided it warranted a product change
Knowing when a concept was technically viable but wrong for the user — no amount of AI output replaces that
